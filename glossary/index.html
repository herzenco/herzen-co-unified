Product leadership glossary

Clear language for complicated product work.

Straight definitions for the terms founders, operators, and delivery teams encounter when a product needs stronger ownership.

Fractional product leadership
Senior product ownership embedded into a team without a full-time executive hire. The work can include roadmap decisions, delivery cadence, stakeholder alignment, product metrics, and accountability for what ships next.
Product roadmap
A living view of product priorities, desired outcomes, sequencing, and tradeoffs. A roadmap explains why work matters and what changes when new evidence appears; it is not simply a dated feature list.
Product backlog
The ordered set of product work a team may pursue. A useful backlog contains clear problems, outcomes, acceptance criteria, and enough context to make prioritization possible.
Minimum viable product (MVP)
The smallest useful version of a product that can test a meaningful assumption with real users. “Minimum” refers to scope, not quality, reliability, or respect for the user.
Product discovery
The work of reducing uncertainty before and during delivery through customer conversations, evidence review, prototypes, technical investigation, and small experiments.
Sprint cadence
The recurring rhythm a team uses to plan, build, review, and learn. The purpose is predictable decision-making and feedback—not adding ceremonies for their own sake.
Delivery risk
Anything that could prevent the team from achieving the intended outcome, including unclear decisions, dependencies, technical uncertainty, capacity constraints, or weak stakeholder alignment.
Product operations
The systems, rituals, information, and coordination that help product teams make and execute decisions consistently. It connects strategy, customer evidence, delivery, and measurement.
